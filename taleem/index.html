Welcome

You're joining Taaleem. Here's everything, in order.

From the day you accept your offer through to your first weeks at school — whenever in the year you are joining.

What this means for you
  • Joining from overseas. If you are eligible for a Taaleem-provided flight, HR will arrange your ticket. HR also applies for your entry permit, and your residence visa is completed in your first 30 days in the country.
  • Already in the UAE. You won't need an entry permit — your status transfers to Taaleem from inside the country. Your current visa has to be cancelled first, and that part is on you.
  • Based in Dubai. Your teaching approval goes through KHDA, and your visa medical is at a Medical Fitness Centre.
  • Based in Abu Dhabi. Your teaching approval goes through ADEK, and your visa medical is at the Etihad Visa Screening Centre.
  • Moving with family. You sponsor their visas yourself once your own is issued. Start the attested marriage and birth certificates now — they are the slowest part.
  • Moving on your own. Nothing to arrange for dependents, so the family visa step won't apply to you.

Safeguarding

Safeguarding is the responsibility of every adult in a Taaleem school, from your first day and whatever your role. You do not need to be an expert — you need to know what to do if you are ever worried about a child.

Never investigate a concern yourself

If something worries you, your job is to notice it, record it and report it — not to work out whether it is true. Don't question the student for more detail, don't approach the family, and don't discuss it with colleagues who aren't part of the reporting process. Well-meant questions can compromise a later inquiry.

Report the same day. Never hold a concern overnight.

If a concern arises

  1. Listen. Let the student speak without interrupting or leading them. Don't promise to keep it secret — you can't.
  2. Reassure them that they were right to tell you.
  3. Record it as soon as you can, in the student's own words where possible, with the date, time and place. Stick to what was said and seen rather than what you concluded.
  4. Report it the same day to your Designated Safeguarding Lead and your Principal or Superintendent. You can raise it through any appropriate route — what matters is that it reaches them quickly.

Never decide a concern is too small to pass on. Judging significance isn't your call, and a small piece may complete a picture someone else already holds.

If the concern involves a member of staff

The duty to report is exactly the same. Where the concern involves the Designated Safeguarding Lead or another senior leader, raise it directly with your Principal or Superintendent. Where that would not be appropriate, raise it with the Head of Safeguarding at Central Office.

There is always a route. If one feels closed to you, another is open.

Confidentiality

You cannot promise a student that you will keep something to yourself. If they ask, explain gently that you may have to tell someone whose job it is to help. Information is shared on a need-to-know basis only — discussing a concern with colleagues who have no role in it is a breach in itself.

Professional boundaries

Keep your relationships with students professional and visible. Communicate through school systems and your school account so there is always a record, rather than personal phone numbers or social media. Avoid being alone with a student out of sight of others; where a one-to-one conversation is necessary, stay somewhere visible and let a colleague know. Don't transport students in your own car, photograph them on a personal device, or give and accept personal gifts.

Physical contact should be limited to what your role genuinely requires — first aid, keeping someone safe, or the demands of subjects like PE, drama and music. If a situation forces you into contact that could be misread, record it and tell a colleague at the time rather than afterwards.

Your documents

You'll receive a request for documentation from your HR representative. What you send back is used for your teaching approval, your visa, your insurance and your personnel file.

Your checklist

The Staff Information Form sent with your Letter of Intent confirms exactly what applies to you, but in almost every case it comes down to these.

  • Passport copy
  • Previous visa, where applicable
  • Attested education certificates
  • Equivalence certificate
  • Police clearance certificate

Check your scans before you send them

Rejected scans are the single most common cause of delay. Every scan must be clear, not blurry and free from glare. Send colour copies as PDF or JPG — photographs of documents and CamScanner files cannot be used for official purposes and are rejected automatically.

Once your documents are processed, your employment contract is issued for signature. That signature is what allows the entry permit application to begin, so the sooner the documents are complete, the sooner everything downstream can move.

References

You need two, covering the last five years of employment. If you've had more than two employers in that time, you'll need more references to cover the full five years.

  • Academic applicants: from your current or previous School Principal
  • Administrative applicants: from your direct Line Manager
  • Not from relatives or friends, and not from a private email address
  • Fully completed, signed and dated by every referee

Referees are asked about your suitability to work with children, your conduct and attendance, any disciplinary procedures, and any substantiated concerns relating to the safety and welfare of children.

Police clearance

A police clearance or criminal background check from your country of residence, always covering the last five years.

The certificate has to be addressed to the right authority

A police clearance certificate is issued for a named body, and one addressed to the wrong authority will not be accepted for your teaching approval — you would have to apply again.

You're joining a Dubai school, so it must be addressed to the Knowledge and Human Development Authority (KHDA). You're joining an Abu Dhabi school, so it must be addressed to the Department of Education and Knowledge (ADEK) rather than KHDA. Select ADEK as the requesting authority when you apply, with Purpose: Work and Language: English.

Attestation

Your education certificates need to be attested — verified by the UAE Embassy and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs — before they are recognised here. Your visa and your teaching approval both depend on it.

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs handles the final attestation. Their service page sets out what's accepted, what it costs and how to submit documents, including the digital route for certificates issued in the UAE.

MOFA document attestation

Which documents

  • Your degree and teaching qualification
  • Equivalence certificate
  • Your marriage certificate
  • Birth certificates for your children
  • A name change certificate, if applicable

Family documents need attesting a second time by the UAE Embassy in order for you to sponsor your dependents. Sponsorship is your responsibility and is done at your own cost — your HR representative will guide you through it.

Attesting before you come

  1. Have your certificates notarised by a solicitor
  2. UK certificates only: legalised by the FCO
  3. Attested by the UAE Embassy in the country the certificates were issued
  4. Bring the documents or copies to the UAE and submit them to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for final attestation

Attesting once you're here

  1. Take the documents to your home country's embassy for authentication
  2. Take them to the Department of Foreign Affairs to verify authenticity
  3. Take them to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in Dubai for final attestation

Alternatively, you can send your documents back to your home country and have the whole process completed there.

Visas and sponsorship

Your HR representative handles most of the visa process for you. This page explains what's happening, and the points where you have to turn up in person.

Working in the UAE requires an employment visa for the whole of your employment. Once you arrive you have a 30-day window to complete it, and HR will move it along within that time.

Working in the UAE requires an employment visa for the whole of your employment. Because you're already in the country, you won't need an entry permit — instead your status is transferred to Taaleem from inside the UAE. The 30-day window starts when your current visa is cancelled.

How it runs, joining from overseas

  1. Your entry permit is processed by HR and emailed to you
  2. You travel to the UAE and complete the relevant immigration process on arrival — your passport or entry permit may not be physically stamped, and HR will tell you what documentation to provide afterwards
  3. HR changes your status from entry permit to residence
  4. You attend a medical — blood test and x-ray, or blood test only if you've lived in Dubai before
  5. You attend an Emirates ID centre for biometrics and an eye scan, which you may be able to skip if you've lived in the UAE before
  6. You return all documents and your passport to HR, who complete the process
  7. Your Emirates ID is issued and handed to you once it reaches the school

How it runs, joining from inside the UAE

  1. Your current visa is cancelled by your existing sponsor. This is the step you have to drive — HR can't begin until the cancellation paperwork exists, so agree the date with your current employer early
  2. You give HR the cancellation document and your passport
  3. HR applies for a change of status inside the country — no entry permit and, in most cases, no need to leave and re-enter
  4. You attend a medical — blood test and x-ray, or blood test only if you've lived in Dubai before
  5. You attend an Emirates ID centre for biometrics and an eye scan, which you may be able to skip if you've held a UAE residence visa before
  6. You return all documents and your passport to HR, who complete the process
  7. Your Emirates ID is issued and handed to you once it reaches the school

Mind the gap between visas

There is usually a short period between your old visa being cancelled and your Taaleem residence visa being issued. Check the grace period that applies to you before you cancel, and tell HR straight away if the dates look tight.

If your passport ever changes, tell HR immediately. Your visa is linked to your Emirates ID, and out-of-date passport details in the immigration system cause problems when you travel.

About the UAE

A federation of seven emirates on the eastern side of the Arabian Peninsula, formed on 2 December 1971. Around 9.9 million people live here, and roughly 88 in every 100 are expatriates.

Language and money

Arabic is the official language, but Arabic and English are both official business languages and a great many others are spoken across the expatriate community. The currency in the UAE is the United Arab Emirates Dirham (AED), divided into 100 fils, with notes from 5 to 1,000.

Climate

A desert climate: mild, pleasant winters and very hot summers, with humidity that makes the heat harder work than the number suggests.

The UAE has a hot climate for much of the year, with particularly high temperatures during the summer months. Please check the expected conditions for your arrival period and plan accordingly.

Rainfall (Dec–Mar)

What little rain there is falls in these months. It is infrequent, but it can arrive as an intense downpour — roads flood quickly because they are not built to drain heavy rain.

Moving to the UAE

Culture and customs

The UAE welcomes newcomers, and it does so with clear expectations. Knowing them makes life here easier from the first week.

Respecting local culture and customs

The UAE is a multicultural country with its own customs and traditions. Please be respectful of local culture and customs, particularly when visiting government buildings and other formal settings.

Dress should be respectful and appropriate for the setting. Standards are more relaxed in some places than others, and what is fine at a beach club is not appropriate at a government office, a mosque or a school.

The UAE government publishes guidance on living here, covering culture, customs and everyday practicalities. If you are ever unsure, ask your buddy — it is exactly the kind of question they are there for.

Information and services on u.ae

What matters here

Hospitality, tolerance, family cohesion and solidarity run through Emirati society, along with real pride in that heritage. After faith, family comes first — children are considered jewels and family structures are close-knit.

The country's roots are in trade, fishing and pearl diving, which was the main source of income for many well into the early twentieth century. That history sits alongside a strong present-day push in science, technology and innovation.

Ramadan

The ninth month of the Islamic calendar, observed as a month of fasting, prayer, reflection and community. The dates vary each year because the Islamic calendar is based on the lunar cycle.

Don't eat, drink or smoke in public during daylight hours. This applies to non-Muslims too, and it's taken seriously — doing so can attract a fine or a reprimand from the police. Restaurants stay open and serve as normal, and most businesses work shorter hours.

Food and drink

An enormous range of cuisines, and supermarkets that cater to a very diverse population. Imported products cost more; local produce is cheap and good. Pork isn't served in most restaurants — designated venues hold pork licences, and selected supermarkets have separately labelled areas.

Code of conduct

Every Taaleem employee commits to a set of professional, behavioural and moral obligations — towards students, parents, colleagues and UAE society. The Code of Conduct is the document that sets them out.

You are required to read it

The Code of Conduct is an internal Taaleem policy, and you are given access to it from the outset. Reading it and familiarising yourself with it is a requirement of your employment, not an optional extra — breaches can result in penalties against you.

It applies from the moment you accept your offer, not from your first day in a classroom.

In broad terms: professional appearance and behaviour, how you conduct yourself with students, conflicts of interest and the use of school resources, alcohol and substances, and confidentiality — including what you may and may not share publicly or on social media.

The policy itself is the authoritative statement of each of these. It sits alongside safeguarding, which carries its own obligations.

Money and banking

You can't open a UAE bank account until your visa and Emirates ID are complete, and you can't be paid without one. You must have enough of your own money to support yourself until your salary and banking are established.

Your salary can only be paid into a UAE account, so the main job here is getting your existing account onto Taaleem's payroll — and funding the gap until the first run yourself.

If you are arranging your own accommodation, budget considerably more

Landlords here also commonly expect rent in one to four cheques for the year, rather than monthly.

When will I receive my first salary?

Your first salary will depend on your joining date and the monthly payroll cut-off. If you join before the payroll cut-off, your salary may be included in that month's payroll. If you join after the payroll cut-off, your first salary may be paid in the following month's payroll.

Please plan your personal finances accordingly and make sure you have sufficient funds to cover your living expenses until your first salary is received.

Your HR team will confirm the position that applies to your own joining date.

Burj Khalifa, the historic souks either side of the creek, Sky Dive Dubai, Global Village, Miracle Garden, and Ski Dubai when the heat wins. The Metro and hop-on buses cover a lot of ground.

Types of leave

Beyond your annual leave, which follows the school calendar, these are the main kinds you should know about.

Sick leave

Maximum 15 calendar days per year

Reporting an absence

Tell your Line Manager as soon as you know you will be absent, and by 6am at the latest on the morning of the absence. This is the requirement across all Taaleem schools. Cover has to be arranged before the school day starts, so the earlier someone knows, the less disruption there is to your students.

Sick leave is for genuine sickness or illness. Other reasons for being away are covered by the leave types below.

This varies by school

Your school's procedure

The 6am deadline applies across the group. Beyond that, each school sets its own procedure — how to notify, who to notify, and which form to complete so cover can be arranged. Some schools require notification by phone rather than by email or message.

Your school will confirm the procedure that applies to you. Check it when you join rather than assuming.

  • A medical certificate is required from day three, and must be an official document from the Dubai or Abu Dhabi Health Authority, at your own expense
  • Under UAE labour law there's no paid sick leave during your probation period
  • Weekends immediately before or after a period of sick leave aren't counted — but if the absence runs across both sides of a weekend, those days do count, and the certificate must cover the whole period including the weekend

Bereavement Leave

Employees may be entitled to 5 working days of bereavement leave where the funeral is held within the country, and up to 10 working days where the funeral is held outside the country, in accordance with the applicable policy.

Taaleem understands that living away from home means a bereavement may require you to travel at short notice.

Maximum 5 working days per year

For the hospitalisation of a dependent, or an embassy or government appointment that can only be taken during working hours. Both Bereavement and Exceptional Circumstances leave are approved by your Principal, following your school's procedure.

Working at Taaleem

School inspections

Schools in the UAE are inspected by their education regulator. If you haven't worked in an inspected school before, this is the general shape of it.

Regulatory requirements

Every Taaleem school operates under the requirements of the education authority for its emirate, and is inspected against that authority's published framework. Inspections look at the quality of teaching and learning, students' attainment and progress, wellbeing, leadership and the school's own improvement planning.

The inspection body, the framework and the way an inspection runs differ depending on which authority regulates your school. Your school will support you in preparing for inspections.

What it means for you

  • Lesson observations may form part of an inspection, and inspectors may speak to staff, students, parents and governors
  • You may be asked to have your planning and relevant assessment data available
  • Your school will brief you on its own preparation and expectations

Inspections judge the school, not you individually

You won't receive a personal judgement grade from an inspection. The outcome is a judgement about the school as a whole.

Afterwards

Inspection outcomes are normally shared with school leaders first, and judgements stay provisional and confidential until the regulator's quality assurance is complete. Reports are usually published on the regulating authority's website in due course.

Unemployment insurance

The ILOE scheme provides income if you lose your job for reasons other than resignation or disciplinary action. Subscription has been mandatory since 1 January 2023 under Federal Decree Law No. 13 of 2022.

You subscribe to this yourself — and failing to do so results in fines

It's mandatory but it isn't automatic. It is not deducted through payroll and Taaleem does not enrol you, so it is every employee's personal responsibility to make sure they are subscribed and that they renew on time. Sort it once your Emirates ID arrives.

What it costs

Your category depends on your basic salary, not your total monthly package.

CategoryBasic salaryPremiumMaximum payout
AAED 16,000 or belowAED 5 + VAT / monthUp to AED 10,000, up to 3 months
BAbove AED 16,000AED 10 + VAT / monthUp to AED 20,000, up to 3 months

You can pay monthly, quarterly, semi-annually, or annually in full.

What you'd receive

60% of your average basic salary over the six months before the job loss, for up to three months — provided you've been subscribed for at least twelve consecutive months, and capped at the figure for your category above.

To subscribe or renew

You need your Emirates ID or Unified Number and a valid UAE mobile number. Note that your Emirates ID number and Unified Number are the same — it appears as the EID on your Emirates ID and as the UID on your visa. The scheme is open to Emiratis and residents working in the private and federal government sectors.

  1. Go to iloe.ae and choose Subscribe Here under Involuntary Loss of Employment.
  2. Choose Non-Registered in MOHRE — not Private. This is the option that applies to Taaleem employees.
  3. Sign in with the OTP. Don't use the Registered User option.
  4. Enter your basic salary only, not your full monthly income. Your basic salary is shown on your payslip.
  5. Complete the payment.
  6. Download the insurance policy from your registered email and save it as a PDF.

You can also subscribe through the ILOE mobile app, exchange centres including Al Ansari, your telecom bill, SMS, bank apps, business centres, kiosks and ATMs.

Send HR your receipt. Save the policy or receipt as a PDF and email it to your HR representative for your file.
